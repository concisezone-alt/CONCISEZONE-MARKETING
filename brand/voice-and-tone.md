# ConciseZone — Voice & Tone Guide

---

## Our Voice

ConciseZone's voice is **clear, confident, and human**. We speak like a smart colleague — not a corporate press release or a tech startup trying too hard.

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What It Means | Example |
|-----------|---------------|---------|
| **Clear** | Simple words, short sentences, no jargon | "Get more done." not "Maximize operational throughput." |
| **Confident** | Direct and assertive, not hedging | "ConciseZone keeps your team aligned." not "ConciseZone might help your team." |
| **Human** | Warm, relatable, never robotic | "We know Mondays are rough." not "Users experience productivity challenges." |
| **Focused** | Say what matters, cut the rest | 30 words, not 100 |

---

## Tone by Channel

Tone adapts by context while the core voice stays consistent.

### Email

- **Tone:** Warm and direct
- **Style:** Conversational, one clear idea per email
- **Avoid:** Overlong intros, multiple CTAs, passive voice

### Blog / Long-Form

- **Tone:** Authoritative but approachable
- **Style:** Teach, don't preach; use examples and data
- **Avoid:** Padding, vague intros, weak conclusions

### Social — LinkedIn

- **Tone:** Professional, insightful, thought leadership
- **Style:** Hook in the first line, short paragraphs, one key insight
- **Avoid:** Self-congratulatory posts, excessive hashtags

### Social — X (Twitter)

- **Tone:** Sharp, punchy, conversational
- **Style:** Under 280 characters; hook-first; one idea
- **Avoid:** Clichés, weak openers ("Excited to announce…")

### Ads

- **Tone:** Direct, benefit-first, urgent
- **Style:** Headline = benefit; body = proof; CTA = action
- **Avoid:** Superlatives without substance ("best-in-class")

## Language Rules

### Use

- Active voice: "You'll finish faster." not "Tasks will be completed faster."
- Contractions: "We're" not "We are" (in casual channels)
- Second person: "You" and "your team"
- Concrete nouns and verbs: "Cut meeting time in half."

### Avoid

- Buzzwords: "synergy", "leverage", "disruptive", "holistic"
- Filler phrases: "In today's fast-paced world…", "At the end of the day…"
- Passive constructions: "It has been shown that…"
- Hedging: "might", "could potentially", "in some cases"

---

## Punctuation & Formatting

- Use em dashes (—) for emphasis breaks, not parentheses
- Sentence case for headlines in content; title case for ads only
- Oxford comma: always
- Exclamation marks: use sparingly (max 1 per piece)

## Examples

### Weak vs. Strong Copy

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| "ConciseZone is a powerful productivity platform that leverages AI to help teams collaborate more effectively." | "Stop juggling tools. ConciseZone keeps your team in sync — automatically." |
| "We are excited to announce our new feature." | "New: your tasks now sort themselves." |
| "Users may experience improved productivity outcomes." | "Teams using ConciseZone save 3 hours a week." |
